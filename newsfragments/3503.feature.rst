``trio.run_process`` now accepts ``stdout_limit`` and ``stderr_limit``
(an integer or a `trio.OutputLimit`) to bound how much captured output is
kept in memory. Overflow can be truncated (keeping the head) or handled
with ``on_overflow="keep_head_tail"`` to keep both ends; the returned
`subprocess.CompletedProcess` and raised `subprocess.CalledProcessError`
gain ``stdout_capture`` / ``stderr_capture`` `trio.CapturedOutput`
objects reporting whether truncation happened and how many bytes were
discarded. Output already captured when the call is cancelled or times
out is now attached to the propagating `trio.Cancelled` (or the
exception group containing it) instead of being lost. The new
`trio.CapturingReceiveStream` provides the same declaration and
accounting when reading a `trio.Process` stream chunk by chunk. Without
a declared limit, collection semantics are unchanged.
